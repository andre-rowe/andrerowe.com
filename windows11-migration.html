Windows 11 Migration in a Hospital System

The problem

A fixed deadline, in buildings that never close

Microsoft ended support for Windows 10 on October 14, 2025. Any Windows 10 machine still in service after that date would collect known, unpatched vulnerabilities while sitting next to patient data.

Windows 10 endpoints across hospital, outpatient and specialty sites had to move to Windows 11. These devices run nursing stations, clinics and specialty practices around the clock, so there were two ways to fail: miss the date and leave unpatched endpoints in clinical spaces, or rush the swaps and take a working station, printer or department application away during patient care.

Environment

Nine sites, two boroughs, one Microsoft-managed endpoint environment

NYU Langone Health, an academic health system with hospital, outpatient and specialty sites. The work happened in two places: the basement staging room, where devices were provisioned in batches and volume mattered, and clinical units, where devices were swapped at the user's station and disruption mattered.

My role

From the basement staging room to the nursing station

I worked at the device and user level. I provisioned devices with Windows Autopilot and operated inside a Microsoft-managed endpoint environment. Alongside on-site swaps, I coordinated Windows 11 migrations that could be completed remotely. A device counted as done only when it was on a supported build, the right person could work on it, and the clinical station was still whole.

  1. Prep

    Matched each device to its user and room on the deployment schedule and confirmed its Autopilot registration.

  2. Stage

    Provisioned devices with Windows Autopilot in batches in the basement staging room: out-of-box experience, Autopilot profile, then the Enrollment Status Page holding setup while required apps and policies installed.

  3. Validate

    Checked user sign-in, mapped drives, printers and required departmental applications before handoff.

  4. Deploy on site

    Swapped devices on clinical units at times that avoided patient care, and confirmed the Wyse thin clients at the same station still connected.

  5. User readiness

    Handed off only when the user confirmed they could do their normal work.

  6. Post-deployment support

    Handled approximately 65 ServiceNow cases a week, handled Active Directory account unlocks, password resets and group membership changes, and closed cases with clear notes and no patient data.

Not claimed: design or configuration of Autopilot profiles, Intune policies, app assignments, Entra ID or BitLocker settings; Active Directory work beyond account-level changes; building the deployment schedule; escalation work beyond the service desk; a lifetime device count; program completion; anything about old devices after the swap.

Constraints, outcome and lessons

What shaped the work, and how it ended

Constraints

  • A fixed external deadline: October 14, 2025
  • No lost workstations mid-shift; swaps fit around patient care
  • No patient data in notes; nothing left a device or appeared in case notes
  • Shared Wyse stations and department printers and applications had to work before handoff
  • A site added mid-assignment: NYU Langone Hospital, Brooklyn, from July 2025
  • Contractor scope: I worked inside a deployment workflow the program provided

Outcome

Completed my assignment through its scheduled end. Before it ended, a manager on another NYU Langone team asked me to join his team on a new contract.

Employment verification and the email asking me to join the other team are on file and available to hiring teams on request. No third-party statement exists yet for this engagement.

What I learned

  • Validation before handoff is cheaper than a case after it
  • In a hospital, the schedule belongs to patient care
  • Identity runs through every step: a device is done only when the right person can sign in with the right access
  • What I would do differently: keep my own daily log of devices staged, devices deployed and issues caught at validation, so I could show totals as well as rates
